Nuclear fusion for ideas

Ask a model for a hundred ideas and you get a hundred answers to the same question. We do something else. We take a problem and open it into a billion possible paths — and send ideas down them.

Along the way each idea changes. It's shaped by tools that influence how it develops, and sometimes it meets another idea travelling from a different direction. They collide, and what comes out is something neither of them was.

One of the tools

Reality Distance controls how far an idea is allowed to drift from what's practical.

Stay too close and it never leaves familiar ground. Go too far and it becomes fantasy. But sometimes it's worth letting an idea travel away for a while — free of what seems possible — and then pulling it back to reality from somewhere else entirely.

That's one tool. There are others.

A gold mine, not a search engine

An enormous space of journeys raises an obvious question: how do you avoid drowning in ideas?

When you're searching for gold, you expect to dig through a lot of dirt. The goal isn't for every idea to be great — it's to reach veins we would never have dug into otherwise. And you don't have to go through the dirt yourself. The system evaluates what it produced and surfaces only what deserves your attention.

The system can go through a mountain of dirt. You should only have to see the gold.

Not every problem deserves the same journey. Some are answered in a few hours. Others are worth far more — and the longer the ideas travel, the wider the ground they cover. The size of the journey follows the size of what's at stake.

This is no longer a vision. We have a working MVP, and early experiments are underway.

The name

Odd means unusual, out of the ordinary, different — not wrong. It's what a good idea often looks like before anyone recognizes it. Fusion is what happens when those odd journeys meet.
